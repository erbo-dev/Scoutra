# Scoutra Product Context

## Purpose

Scoutra is an AI-assisted youth-football platform for measurable player assessment, development tracking, academy match analysis, talent discovery, and scouting. Its social mission is to reduce the chance that a capable young player remains invisible simply because they live outside a professional football system.

The product must remain evidence-led and human-centered. Scoutra does not claim to identify the next superstar, replace scouts, or assign a definitive talent score. It helps players, coaches, academies, parents, and scouts understand performance, track development, and reduce the search space that professionals must review.

## Phase 1 Visual Baseline

Phase 1 is not the final Scoutra brand or product interface. It independently reconstructs the visual structure and editorial character of the public Tonsser homepage using original code and local placeholder media. The goal is a premium youth-football marketing foundation with reusable sections, strong responsive behavior, disciplined typography, and replaceable media.

No Tonsser source code, proprietary media, logos, fonts, or runtime dependencies may be embedded. Final Scoutra colors, Kazakhstan-specific storytelling, computer-vision overlays, dashboards, Player Passport interfaces, and Veo footage belong to later phases.

## Product Entry Modes

### Mode A: Open Player Assessment

This mode is for young footballers who may not belong to an academy. A player should be able to complete standardized assessments with a smartphone, a ball, a small training space, and simple markers or cones.

Initial controlled assessment types include:

- Sprint: elapsed time, acceleration, and movement.
- Agility: change of direction, completion time, and movement quality.
- Dribbling: completion time, errors, control, and consistency.
- Passing: target accuracy, execution speed, consistency, and left/right-foot usage.
- First touch: execution in a standardized receiving task.

Controlled protocols are essential because Scoutra must not imply that arbitrary handheld match footage can reliably produce professional full-match statistics.

### Mode B: Academy Match Analysis

This mode is for academies recording matches from a reasonably elevated position with much of the pitch visible. A future pipeline may include video ingestion, player detection, multi-object tracking, ball detection, pitch calibration, player identity, event extraction, football metrics, and player reporting.

Potential metrics include touches, passes, pass completion, ball losses, recoveries, interceptions, tackles, progressive actions, positioning, movement, and heatmaps. More advanced metrics may follow only when their reliability is defensible. AI supplies additional objective information; the coach retains final judgment.

## Player Passport

Both product modes converge into the Player Passport. It must not be a shallow FIFA-style card with unexplained pace, passing, or overall ratings.

Significant metrics should preserve provenance:

1. Metric and value.
2. Source session.
3. Video evidence.
4. Date.
5. Measurement method.
6. Confidence or evidence quality.
7. Historical values.

A future passport may show an anonymous player identifier, age group, position, region, percentile-based measurements, verified-session count, and recent progress. The central promise is that users can understand where a measurement came from.

## Development Trajectory

Scoutra is not a one-time assessment that produces a permanent score. It tracks performance through time and distinguishes between current level, trajectory, consistency, and evidence quality.

Two players may reach the same present value through very different histories. A steady plateau and a rapid improvement curve should not be treated as identical. Development trajectory is informative, but it must not be marketed as a prediction of professional success.

The core development loop is:

1. Assess.
2. Understand.
3. Train.
4. Retest.
5. Track progress.
6. Adjust.

Future recommendations may point players toward wall first-touch work, directional receiving, two-foot passing, scanning before receiving, pressure passing, or agility work. The goal is development rather than gamification for its own sake.

## Talent Discovery

Scouts and academies need search and evidence review rather than a simplistic global leaderboard. Useful filters may include age, position, region, acceleration percentile, passing percentile, minimum verified sessions, and recent development direction.

Search results should summarize evidence-backed measurements and progression. A scout can open a profile, inspect source sessions, watch evidence, review progress, add a player to a shortlist, and potentially initiate a trial request.

Scoutra does not decide that a child is talented. It helps professionals identify which players in a very large pool may be worth evaluating personally.

## Users and Needs

### Player

- Understand performance.
- Improve through targeted work.
- Track development.
- Build credible visibility.

### Coach

- Reduce manual analysis time.
- Understand match and assessment performance.
- Track player development.
- Retain decision authority.

### Scout or Academy

- Search a broad player pool.
- Reduce the initial review set.
- Inspect evidence and progression.
- Build shortlists and issue controlled invitations.

### Parent or Guardian

- Understand development information.
- See how measurements were produced.
- Maintain safety and visibility.
- Control trial and contact requests.

## Safety for Minors

Scoutra must never resemble an unrestricted adult-to-child social network. A child's direct phone number should not be publicly exposed.

The desired interaction model is:

1. Verified scout, club, academy, or coach submits a trial request.
2. The request goes to the parent or guardian.
3. The guardian approves or rejects it.
4. Further communication occurs only after approval.

Future professional account types may include Verified Academy, Verified Club, Verified Scout, and Verified Coach.

## Full Player Journey

Discover → Assess → Understand → Develop → Retest → Track → Verify → Player Passport → Scout discovery → Trial request → Parent approval → Trial → Academy → Match analytics → Continuous development.

The brand logic is: Measure → Understand → Improve → Verify → Discover.

## MVP Direction

### Individual MVP

- Sprint, agility, dribbling, and passing assessments.
- Simple Player Passport.
- Progression history.

### Academy MVP

- Player and basic ball tracking.
- Touches and selected passing events.
- Losses and reliable recoveries or interceptions.
- Heatmap and simple match report.

### Scout MVP

- Player list and filters.
- Profile and verified results.
- Assessment video and progress graph.
- Shortlist.

## Business Direction

The likely primary model is B2B. Academies may pay for match analysis, team dashboards, player profiles, historical progress, reports, talent discovery, and shortlist tools.

Players may use a freemium model. A free tier should preserve basic access to assessments, profiles, and development history because accessibility is part of the mission. A future paid player tier may include broader analytics, additional assessment types, richer development insight, and advanced reports.

## Product Principles

- Do not claim that AI can identify the next Messi.
- Do not claim that AI replaces scouts or coaches.
- Do not claim that a single rating determines football talent.
- Explain evidence, method, confidence, and history.
- Keep final evaluation human.
- Protect minors through verified roles and guardian-controlled contact.
- Favor measurable development over empty gamification.
- Keep basic player participation accessible.

## Future Visual Direction

The final product should feel like premium sports editorial design combined with youth football, modern product design, and subtle computer vision. It should not resemble generic AI SaaS, purple-gradient startup branding, crypto dashboards, neon laboratories, FIFA Ultimate Team fandom, or a children's football-school website.

Future media should feature recurring fictional Kazakh youth players and Kazakhstan or Central Asian football environments. Likely replaceable assets include hero football video, player groups, standardized test footage, training progress, academy matches, scouts, and final player portraits.

Future product imagery may introduce subtle tracking overlays and interfaces for the Player Passport, Scout Search, and Academy Analytics, but Phase 1 intentionally avoids those final Scoutra elements.
